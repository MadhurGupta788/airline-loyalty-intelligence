> **Summer Projects '26 | Consulting & Analytics Club, IIT Guwahati**  
> Track: Strategy | Data Analytics

---

## Problem

Airlines use loyalty programs to drive repeat business, but most programs react too late — high-value customers go silent long before formally cancelling. This project builds a proactive, data-driven loyalty intelligence system over ~16,700 Canadian loyalty members (2012–2018) to answer:

- Which members are likely to disengage, and when?
- Which members are genuinely valuable, not just historically?
- What specific retention interventions should marketing act on?

## Approach

### Churn definition
Two competing definitions are built and compared in `01_eda.ipynb`:
- **Definition A (formal):** explicit membership cancellation
- **Definition B (behavioral):** active in H1 2017 but zero flights from Jul 2017 onward ← *primary label*

Definition B is preferred because it captures the commercially dangerous pattern of silent disengagement before formal cancellation.

### Modelling
- Baseline: Logistic Regression
- Primary: XGBoost classifier with SHAP explainability
- Evaluation: AUC-ROC, Precision-Recall, F1 (stratified split, class-weighted for imbalance)

### Segmentation
K-means on behavioral features (active months, redemption rate, recency, seasonal concentration), profiled against CLV and churn risk to produce actionable marketing segments.

## Key findings
*(updated as notebooks are completed)*

- ~12.4% of members show behavioral churn (silent disengagement)
- Cancelled members have ~3× lower average monthly flight activity than active members
- 1,570 members had zero flights across all of 2017–2018 despite being enrolled
